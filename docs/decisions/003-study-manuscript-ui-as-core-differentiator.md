# Decision 003: Study Manuscript UI as a Core Differentiator

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-05-23

## Context

Many digital Bible tools allow notes, highlights, and bookmarks, but often place them in detached panels, hidden drawers, or flat lists.

The founder's product direction is different.

The Bible text itself should become a living study manuscript that visibly retains the user's annotations and reflections.

## Decision

Study Manuscript Mode should be treated as a core differentiator of the product.

The app should support:
- highlights
- underlines
- handwritten-style margin notes
- anchored note placement
- visual persistence of annotations on the page
- a reading surface that feels like a personal annotated Bible

## Why This Decision Was Made

This approach strengthens the emotional, spiritual, and practical connection between reading and study.

Instead of feeling like a reading app with disconnected note features, the app should feel like a Bible manuscript that the reader has genuinely studied and marked over time.

## Consequences

### Positive
- stronger product identity
- more memorable reading/study experience
- better continuity between reading and reflection
- deeper personal ownership of study content

### Tradeoffs
- annotation anchoring and layout are more complex to implement
- visual clutter must be carefully controlled
- the design system must preserve reverence and readability

## Revisit Conditions

This decision should be revisited only if the manuscript-style approach proves too visually distracting or too difficult for users to manage in practice.
