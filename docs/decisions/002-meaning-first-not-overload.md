# Decision 002: Meaning First, Not Information Overload

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-05-23

## Context

Many Bible study tools are powerful, but they often overwhelm readers with too much information too quickly.

The product direction for Logos Incarnate is not to compete by exposing more raw information than other tools.

Instead, the app should guide readers into the core depth of meaning with clarity first and controlled expansion later.

## Decision

The product should be designed according to a meaning-first model.

This means:
- default experiences should be concise and understandable
- deeper technical or lexical detail should be expandable, not dominant
- the interface should prioritize insight over data density
- users should understand what matters before seeing everything available

## Why This Decision Was Made

This decision reflects the founder's conviction that too much information often weakens understanding rather than strengthening it.

The goal of the product is not merely to expose biblical study data, but to help readers understand words, people, places, themes, and passages in a meaningful way.

## Consequences

### Positive
- better clarity for readers
- stronger product differentiation
- reduced interface overload
- more accessible entry point for both beginners and serious learners

### Tradeoffs
- some advanced users may initially want more raw detail visible by default
- curation and ranking become more important product responsibilities

## Revisit Conditions

This decision should be revisited only if user testing consistently shows that the default presentation is too shallow or blocks meaningful study progress.
